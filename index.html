Hybrid Plan — 2 Gym Days + Daily Micro-Sessions

Goal: Maintain/slightly improve strength and body composition on ~4 hrs/week of gym time, while protecting the bulk of the week for kernel study. Fat loss is secondary — nutrition and consistency, not extra training volume, are what carry it forward.

Weekly Pattern Check

Movement / Quality Frequency & Implementation
Squat 2× (Heavy Wed, lighter front squat Sat)
Hinge 2× (RDL Wed, deadlift Sat)
Push 1× heavy (Wed) + daily push-up/dip GTG
Pull 1× heavy (Sat) + daily pull-up GTG
Power/Speed Sprints 2×/week, jumps 1×/week
Conditioning Jump rope 2×/week

The Logic: Wed = squat + heavy push, Sat = deadlift + heavy pull + power. Every major pattern still gets hit at least once heavy per week, with a lighter second exposure folded into the other day. Two hard sessions with 72+ hrs recovery means you can push RPE 7–8 harder. Micro-sessions cost 15 minutes and zero recovery debt.

Strategies to Make This Stick

  • Protect the 2 gym days like a meeting: With only 4 hrs/week of lifting, missing one session is a 50% cut for that week — much higher stakes per session than when you had 6 to buffer against.
  • Log every session: At low frequency, progressive overload has to come from small, deliberate jumps (weight, reps) tracked week to week. The log is what tells you it's working.
  • Keep GTG sets genuinely sub-max: The whole point of grease-the-groove is skill/strength maintenance without fatigue cost. Grinding reps turns a 5-minute set into a second workout that eats study energy.
  • Nutrition is your main fat-loss lever: You're cutting weekly training volume by more than half. Fat loss must come from a modest calorie deficit and enough protein (~1.6–2.2 g/kg, so ~105–145 g/day at your weight).
  • Habit-stack the micro-sessions: Attach them to kernel study blocks (e.g., rope or sprints as a break between reading sessions) rather than treating them as separate to-do items.
  • Check InBody every 2 weeks: Checking too often adds noise and second-guessing to a plan meant to run on autopilot.
  • Adjust expectations: Fat-loss pace will slow, and that is fine. The goal is "don't lose the muscle and conditioning you built," with fat loss as a slow, diet-driven byproduct.
  • Build in a floor, not an all-or-nothing: If a micro-session gets crowded out by a rough coding day, do 2 minutes of pull-ups instead of 15 minutes of rope. Keeping the streak alive matters most.
